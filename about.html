About

A father and son working up the ladder, publishing what we learn on the way — including the expensive mistakes.

Why this exists

The gap

Grassroots motorsport runs on tribal knowledge. The information that decides your season — which chassis is quick at your track, which class is growing, who actually stocks parts — lives in paddock conversations and nowhere else. Meanwhile the information that's easy to find is the information that matters least.

Racers in better-resourced series have engineers, data, and databases. Everybody below that is working from forum threads and whoever sounds most confident. That gap is the whole reason for this site.

We're not a manufacturer and we're not a shop. We're buying this equipment with our own money and racing it, which means we have the same incentive you do: get it right the first time, because the second time costs another few thousand dollars.

How we work

Editorial stance
01
We filter, we don't rank

In most classes half a dozen chassis will win. Naming a single best one would be wrong most of the time. We narrow the field and tell you what decides the rest.

02
Missing data stays missing

When a builder doesn't publish a spec, we say so rather than guessing. A blank field is information about the manufacturer, not a mark against the product.

03
Local support beats spec sheets

The fastest kart is usually the one somebody in your paddock can hand you a part for. We weight regional support as heavily as anything mechanical.

04
No paid placement

Nobody buys a recommendation here. If that ever changes, it will be labelled on the page, not buried in a disclosure link.

Corrections

Tell us

Specs change, prices move, and dealers come and go. If something here is wrong — especially a tubing spec, a price, a dealer listing, or a track's class lineup — we want to know. Being corrected in public is cheaper than being confidently wrong in private.
