Know what you're driving to before you load the trailer

Tracks

Every entry is tagged by what kind of racing actually happens there, and which national series visit. A competition paddock and a birthday-party track both call themselves go-kart tracks. They are not the same place and you should never confuse the two at 6am with a trailer hitched.

Competition

Bring your own kart. Sanctioned club, regional or national racing with tech inspection, published class rules, and a points season. Speeds from 45 to 90+ mph.

League

Track-owned karts, but organised racing — timed heats, standings, adult equipment. Real lap times, no trailer required. A legitimate place to learn racecraft cheaply.

Amusement

Family entertainment. Slow karts, restricted speeds, no championship. Listed so you can find one deliberately — or make sure you don't drive four hours to one by accident.

Coverage, honestly

Seeded from the 2026 national series calendars — CKNA, Route 66, USPKS, SKUSA and Challenge of the Americas. That means every track here demonstrably runs real competition, but it also means the Northeast and South Central are thin, because those regions host regional rather than national rounds. Those gaps are ours, not the sport's.

Why classes go stale

Class lineups change every season and sometimes mid-season. Where we list the national series a track hosts but not its weekly classes, that's because we've confirmed the first and not the second. Always check the track's own schedule before entering.

Run a track?

Send us your surface, your current class list, and your schedule and we'll list it accurately. Corrections are free and we'd rather hear it from you than guess.
