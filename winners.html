How to read this, and how not to

Every row comes from a published race report and links back to it. Where a result was provisional at the time of the report, or changed in technical inspection, it says so on the row.

The tally counts main event wins by chassis brand, and it is not a ranking of which chassis is fastest. Entry numbers vary wildly by brand — a marque with thirty karts in a class will collect more wins than one with three, without being any quicker. Team quality, engine builder and driver matter more than the frame in most of these results. What the tally is genuinely good for is telling you which equipment has real support in the national paddock, because that is what determines whether you can get parts and setup help at your track.

Coverage is strongest where reporting is: USPKS publishes a full race report for every class, so those rounds are complete from Micro Swift up. SKUSA and CKNA are partial for now and we're filling them in. Briggs 206 is the thinnest — it's the biggest class in the country by entries but most of it runs at club and divisional level where nobody writes a race report, so the national events are all that's visible from the outside. Sprint karting only, no dirt or road racing.

One thing the 206 numbers will show once they fill out: the chassis brands that win on 206 are largely not the ones that win in the two-stroke classes. Different frame stiffness suits nine horsepower, and the national two-stroke tally tells you almost nothing about what to buy for a 206 grid.
