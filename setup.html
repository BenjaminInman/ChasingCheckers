A starting point, not an answer

The setup sheet.

Set your class, your track, and the weather. This works out your gearing, gives you a tire pressure window, and tells you which direction to move the chassis. What it will not do is pretend to know what's fast at a track it has never seen.

Read this before you trust a number

Gearing here is arithmetic — ratio, rollout and rev limit give a theoretical top speed, and that part is simply true. Everything else is a starting window drawn from how karts generally behave, not from data at your track on your day.

Two identical karts on the same afternoon will want different sheets because of driver weight, driving style, and how much rubber went down in the session before yours. Use this to stop guessing from zero. Then change one thing at a time and let the stopwatch overrule it.

Measure it rather than guessing. Mark the tire and the ground, roll the kart one full revolution with your weight in the seat, measure between the marks. It changes with pressure, wear and heat, and it moves your speed numbers more than people expect.

The only gearing test that counts

You want to reach the limiter just before you lift at the end of the longest straight — not halfway down it, and not never. If you hit it early you're giving away top speed. If you never get there you're giving away drive off the corner.

Change one thing

Two changes at once means you learn nothing, because you can't tell which one did it. This is the single most expensive habit in club racing, and it costs weekends rather than dollars.

Where these numbers came from

Gearing is arithmetic. Pressure windows and chassis direction are general kart behaviour, stated as ranges because that's what they honestly are. Where we don't have a defensible number for your class, we leave it blank rather than inventing one.

Tell us we're wrong

If a window here is off for your class or your region, we want to hear it. Corrections beat confidence, and this page in particular will get better the more people push back on it.
